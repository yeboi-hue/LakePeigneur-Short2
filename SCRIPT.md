# Lake Peigneur — voice script and shot beats

## Factual basis

- November 20, 1980: drilling associated with Texaco penetrated the Jefferson Island salt mine beneath Lake Peigneur.
- The lake drained into the mine and a large whirlpool formed.
- Fifty miners escaped; seven workers left the drilling rig. No fatalities were reported.
- Eleven barges, a tugboat, drilling equipment and shoreline features were drawn into the collapse area.
- The Delcambre Canal reversed direction as Gulf water flowed back toward the lake; nine barges later resurfaced.

Sources: University of Louisiana at Lafayette Special Collections; Mine Safety and Health Administration, *Report of Mine Inundation*; UPI contemporary reporting (linked in README.md).

## Narration

1. A lake once swallowed an oil rig, eleven barges, and itself. Then it came back as a completely different lake.
2. November twentieth, nineteen eighty. Lake Peigneur in Louisiana is calm, shallow, and dotted with boats.
3. But nearly fifteen hundred feet below, a salt mine stretches beneath the lake. Fifty miners are working underground.
4. Above them, a Texaco drilling crew searches for oil from a platform floating on the water. Then the drill gets stuck.
5. The crew notices something is badly wrong. Their platform starts tilting, so they scramble to shore just in time.
6. Then the lake begins spinning. A huge whirlpool opens, and the drilling rig slides toward the growing hole.
7. The drill has broken into the salt mine. Water pours into the tunnels, flooding the mine and forcing it to close.
8. The current becomes so powerful that the nearby canal reverses direction, pulling water in from the Gulf of Mexico.
9. The lake drains away, taking barges, a tugboat, equipment, and part of the shoreline. But all fifty miners escape safely.
10. Over the next days, the canal refills the basin. Nine barges even rise back to the surface. The lake is now far deeper, and salty.
